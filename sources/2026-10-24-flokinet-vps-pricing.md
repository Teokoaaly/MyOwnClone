A **VPS or Virtual Private Server** is a hosting service based on emulated server hardware. In this environment you receive a static IPv4 + /64 IPv6, together with the tools to control boot, attach ISOs and create backups. It's ready to install and configure Linux

Note this service comes without any applications, such as HTTP servers, Databases, etc. They must be installed and configured by you according to your project's needs.

VPSes are the next step from Shared Hosting accounts and it is designed for clients who are looking for:

- more processing resources
- controlling many more aspects of their server
- ensuring that their data is more protected
- configuring/installing applications as technically required without technical restrictions

All VPSes provided are based on **enterprise SSD only** . The flash memory allows access to data much faster than HDDs.

We are doing our best to offer the latest in performance, by upgrading to NVMe PCIe 8x and U.2 NVMe as storage devices in all our locations.

By using enterprise SSD as the storage device, we aim to provide our customers with the latest in performance and reliability as they are optimized for heavy workloads and long endurance.

A **VPS is a virtualized dedicated server** , so not a bare-metal server with dedicated hardware. Instead it runs in an emulated hardware environment, with all the tools needed for managing it in isolation.

In **a dedicated server** , all the hardware is dedicated to the environment being built by you and the physical isolation provides an even higher level of security, control and stability. Dedicated Servers are in general recommended for clients who need more resources than what is available for VPS clients.

VPSes are limited to a certain amount of resources that can be added. For example, if the hardware cap set by FlokiNET is 10CPU core, 10GB RAM and 240GB you will not be able to upgrade beyond that, while Dedicated Servers are only limited by the motherboard's compatibility/capability and you are able to choose a more detailed hardware configuration (e.g. HDD/SSD/NVMe, 2/4 CPU sockets, DDR3/DDR4/DDR5, GPUs, etc).

VPSes share some resources, and in theory this service can be subject to swings in resource availability. FlokiNET monitors all VPS nodes closely to ensure that these fluctuations are well within the limits and do not affect the individual performance of each VPS. On the other hand, dedicated servers do not share any resources so these fluctuations do not exist, and all hardware capability is available at all times.

Dedicated Servers also allow for options not available to VPSes:

- Dedicated firewall
- Up-link connections up to 100Gbps
- Special storage options such as HW-RAID controllers and Storage Enclosures

Additionally, we recommend you also read the next question "What are SSH keys? Do I need them?" before purchasing your VPS.

SSH keys are a method of authentication used to identify the client and server, and to encrypt the data that is exchanged between them. The pair is generated locally on your end by using the "ssh-keygen" command on linux and an SSH client in Windows such as putty.

Your public key is added into the server while the private key is kept safe on your end. When you attempt to connect to your server, SSH uses the added public key to encrypt a message and sends it to your SSH client, which will use the private key to decrypt and authenticate/allow the connection.

SSH keys provide a much higher security standard than passwords, and are the recommend approach for authentication.

You are not required to have these keys, but during the ordering process, you will be prompted with a PUBLIC SSH key field and an operating system option. Your VPS will deployed with SSH key authentication only, so if you have not provided one, you will have to access the client area >> My services >> select your VPS >> noVNC console - there you will have access to the management console of your VPS.

There, you can login with the username and password chosen at check-out to allow password authentication. This is easily doable by simply editing /etc/ssh/sshd_config to set "PasswordAuthentication" to "yes" and run "sudo systemctl restart sshd" to complete the process.

There are several advantages of using a VPS in comparison with other environments:

- **Flexibility:** Complete control over the operating system, applications, and resources of your server, allows you to configure it to meet your technical requirements. For example, if you wish to install a application that is not supported by Shared-Hosting and beyond that, have any application completely under your control for tweaking according to your application needs
- **Performance:** While you will be sharing resources with other users, the virtualization environment will guarantee a much better performance, by isolating this usage per user
- **Scalability:** Scale your resources on-demand, with minimal downtime. Dedicated servers, on the other hand, offer scalability by adding or removing hardware components, and this process is more complex
- **Isolation:** The virtualization environment includes its own operating system, software, and resources, which are isolated from other virtual machines on the same physical server. This provides a more stable and secure environment
Additionally it's worth noting that dedicated servers offer more raw power and control over the hardware. This can be beneficial for certain types of workloads such as high-performance computing, video rendering or gaming servers.

Overall, VPS will give you a great balance of flexibility, performance, scalability, security, and cost-effectiveness, making it a popular choice for a wide range of websites and applications.
