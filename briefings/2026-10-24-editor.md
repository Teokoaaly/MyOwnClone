## One Thing Worth Your Attention Today

**The cheapest plans are the ones with no exit — and the vault's cost model has no line for exit cost.**

Three independent findings from this batch converge on a single rule that the price-led reading of this market keeps missing:

1. **No provider in this tier allows a downgrade.** BuyVM: "We only allow upgrading to a bigger plan. Downgrading can lead to data loss if the drive shrink fails." ExtraVM: "Due to technical limitations, we cannot downgrade your VPS plan" — upgrade is manual and human, proration is by hand. HOSTKEY's "VDS with GPU" sells a physical GPU while CPU and RAM stay shared with every other VM on the host. This is not one provider being conservative; it is the entire segment.
2. **Backup policy — the thing that makes a downgrade survivable — varies from free to unsourced.** Evoxt and HOSTKEY give weekly offsite backups at zero cost. BuyVM charges 10% of base price (with a $0.50/month floor). HostBrr sells it as an add-on. Skhron offers no managed backups or snapshots at all. The LowEndBox providers advertise "regular backups" in prose with **no policy and no retention published**.
3. **The cheapest exit is via API, and only four providers publish one.** BuyVM's Stallion is explicitly SolusVM-compatible (portable automation); Evoxt publishes a documented API; HostBrr runs VirtFusion; HOSTKEY has an API and panel. ExtraVM, Skhron and every LowEndBox provider publish no panel and no API in the captures.

Combined: **the plan you can leave cheaply is not the plan you can leave expensively — but only if you chose it with the API and the backup policy in mind, before paying.** With annual prepayment as the dominant format in this tier, a mis-sized plan is a 12-month bill, and a non-downgradable provider cannot refund it.

**What to do today:** the vault's ≤ €4/month question now has a documented answer — the **2 GB annual LowEndBox tier** ($16.88–$23.88/year) is the first capacity point that admits Postgres + Redis next to the Flask/Next.js deployment. Before treating that as settled, answer three questions the captures make answerable and that no price comparison in the vault covers:

- **Which provider's automation can actually migrate my data out?** (Stallion/SolusVM, Evoxt API, VirtFusion, or nothing.)
- **What is my backup and my rollback path on that specific plan?** (Free weekly, 10% of base, add-on, or unstated.)
- **Can I get an IPv4 and reach the outside world from it?** (Skhron's IPv4 is €0.65/month and NAT64 is outbound-only; ExtraVM's Sydney has no native DDoS protection; Skhron has no per-client DDoS mitigation at all.)

The vault has spent this week measuring *entry* cost with real precision. The unmeasured quantity — and the one that decides whether a cheap plan is cheap or a 12-month trap — is **cost of leaving**.
