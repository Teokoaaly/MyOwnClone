## The Three Things Worth Your Attention

### 1. The €4/month budget is reachable — but only on plans that can't run the stack

The vault's stated cap is ≤ €4/month. The 2026-10-23 captures produce a full ladder of providers that fit it *on price*: Skhron €2.34, Evoxt $2.99, ExtraVM's $4.50 headline, HostBrr €2.00, LowEndBox at ~$17–24/year. And on **capacity** almost none of them work. The three thresholds the captures make visible:

| Plan | Specs | Fits the stack? |
|---|---|---|
| Skhron entry | 512 MiB / 0.06 vCPU | No |
| LowEndBox $1 tier | 768 MB – 1 GB | No |
| LowEndBox $2 tier (RackNerd $20,98/yr, CloudServer $23,88/yr) | 2 GB / 2 vCPU | Yes — first tier that admits Postgres + Redis next to the Flask/Next.js deployment |
| Hetzner CPX02 | 1 vCPU / 2 GB, €5.99/mo | Yes, but over budget |

So the real answer to "can we host MyOwnClone for ≤ €4/month" is: **yes, at the 2 GB tier of the annual LowEndBox plans, and only there** — with the caveats in §2. New thread: `3-threads/escala-de-hosting-barato-y-el-limite-de-4-euros.md`.

### 2. The cheapest headline prices are the least real ones

- **ExtraVM**: "$4.50 starting at" is a Sold Out plan. The cheapest purchasable is **$8.00/mo**, and only 5 of 14 plans have stock.
- **Evoxt**: the "Plans" and "Benchmark Score" tables give different prices for the same plan (VM-1: $5.99 vs $9.99), and no billing period is published.
- **LowEndBox**: the $1 article claims a ~$120/yr floor; the $2 article publishes $16.88–$23.88/yr. Both articles are editorial roundups with **coupons that have no expiry date and stock that cannot be verified** — prices of a page, not of a contract.
- **Hostkey** publishes no VPS table at all (dedicated servers from €23 only).
- **BuyVM** sells "Dedicated KVM Slices" with "Absolutely no overselling" while the same page states "Fair Share CPU Usage" on 3 of 4 slices.

### 3. Two providers contradict themselves, and one of them excludes Spain

- **Hostkey**: the body of the page requires KYC for all customers and routes crypto through BitPay; the FAQ on the same URL says identity verification is usually not required. Both claims are in the capture. This is a genuine contradiction, not a reading error.
- **NoOnes**: prose says level 2 has "no daily or lifetime limits whatsoever"; the table on the same page says Daily Limit = $100K.
- **NoOnes does not serve Spain** (30 countries removed under MiCA since July 2026) and explicitly forbids the technique the vault's SMS playbook relies on (IP/VPN location matching the number's country). The vault operates in ES — this closes that door.

Separately: **paying in crypto does not imply a privacy posture.** The captures show a full scale — HostBrr (EUR/USD/PayPal/crypto) → Evoxt → ExtraVM (no-KYC) → Skhron (no-KYC, .onion, non-custodial gateway) → Hostkey (KYC mandatory) → NoOnes (KYC by level) — and crypto payment appears on both ends of it. The argument DataImpulse makes for its own privacy is *first-party network*, not crypto.
