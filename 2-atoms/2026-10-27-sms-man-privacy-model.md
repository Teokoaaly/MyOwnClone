## Claim

SMS-MAN requires only an email address for signup and guarantees that no one but the user will have access to the virtual number, with rented numbers not sold to anyone else after the rental period ends.

## Why It Matters

Unlike services that require ID information or passport data during registration, SMS-MAN's email-only signup lowers the barrier to entry and minimizes personal data exposure. The guarantee that rented numbers remain exclusive to the renter (not sold after rental ends) addresses a primary concern with temporary phone number services — that the number could be reused or exposed to third parties later. This model is particularly valuable for users who need verification without compromising their permanent identity or who are concerned about long-term number tracking across digital services.

## [FRICTION]

> [[2026-10-27-receive-smss-privacy]] asserts that Receive-smss protects user privacy by ensuring the temporary phone number is not associated with the user's personal accounts or used for sales calls and texts. SMS-MAN goes further by guaranteeing that rented numbers are not sold to anyone else after the rental period ends, whereas Receive-smss focuses on preventing the number from being linked to the user's permanent identity. Both provide privacy-forward temporary contact, but SMS-MAN's post-rental exclusivity guarantee adds a dimension of long-term number sovereignty that Receive-smss does not explicitly address. Both can be correct — they design for different privacy concerns (number association versus post-rental reuse), but the explicit "not sold" guarantee in SMS-MAN vs. the unspecified post-rental handling in Receive-smss means the friction around long-term number sovereignty remains unresolved.
