## Claim

Anthropic offers four individual consumer/team pricing tiers: **Free** (no cost, for everyone), **Pro** ($20/month billed monthly, $17/month billed annually at $200 up front), **Max** ($100/month, for power users), and **Team** starting at $25/seat/month annually ($25 if billed monthly). The Free plan provides basic access to Claude on web/desktop/mobile with limited features. Pro adds Claude Code, dashboards, science, projects, more models, Chrome and Microsoft 365 integration. Max provides 5x or 20x the usage of Pro per 5-hour session, higher output limits, early access to advanced features, and $100–$200 in API credits.

## Why It Matters

The tiered structure captures different user segments from casual users to power users and teams. Free serves as a frictionless entry point, Pro converts everyday workers, Max targets heavy daily users, and Team addresses organizational needs. The price differential ($0 → $17 → $100 → $25/seat) reflects the value gradient of added capabilities like Claude Code, dashboards, projects, and enterprise-grade integrations.

## [FRICTION]

> The Max plan ranges from $100 to $200/month depending on billing cycle, but the pricing page lists only "$100 Per month" without clarifying that the $200 tier (monthly billing, no annual option shown) exists. The Team plan similarly shows "$25/seat/month if billed annually. $25 if billed monthly" — the annual discount is less pronounced than Pro's (Pro: $200 up front vs $20/month; Team: annual vs monthly both at $25). This ambiguity in how billing cycles affect total cost creates friction for users comparing total cost of ownership across plans.

> The Free plan lists "Free for everyone" but requires login via `https://claude.ai/redirect/...?plan=free`, creating a sign-up barrier that contradicts the "free for everyone" claim. Users expecting zero-friction access encounter an authentication wall before experiencing the product.

> Feature parity across plans is overstated: the comparison table shows "Yes" for many features (Claude Design, Slides, Docs) with "More information" links, suggesting actual capability gaps or restrictions that are not immediately visible. A user cannot verify what "More information" reveals without leaving the pricing page.
